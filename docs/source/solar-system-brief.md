A realistic solar system is anchored by a massive host star containing over 99% of the system's mass, around which unique planets orbit along distinct elliptical paths rather than perfect circles, dynamically accelerating at their closest approach (perihelion) and slowing down at their farthest (aphelion). For cosmic realism, each orbital ring should host only one dominant planet—or at most a massive planet paired with a smaller companion tucked 60 degrees ahead or behind it in a stable Lagrange point—while any major moons must remain securely within their planet’s gravitational Hill Sphere and outside its destructive tidal Roche Limit to avoid being torn into rings. Splitting the entire system is a distinct thermal boundary called the frost line; rocky terrestrial planets form in the hot interior, while volatile gas and ice giants thrive in the freezing outer regions, with the remaining cosmic leftovers organized into an inner rocky asteroid belt, chaotic dust clouds, scattered centaurs, and an outermost freezing reservoir of icy comets like a Kuiper Belt or Oort Cloud.

What a Solar System Consists Of

To build a complete, detailed world, your simulator should populate its space with these distinct structural elements:

- The Host Star: The gravitational anchor of the entire system. Its mass dictates the speed and distance of all orbiting bodies.
- Terrestrial Planets: Small, high-density worlds made of rock and metal (like Earth or Mars) that form close to the star where it is too hot for gases to condense.
- Gas & Ice Giants: Massive, low-density worlds made of hydrogen, helium, methane, and ammonia (like Jupiter or Neptune) that form in the freezing outer system.
- Moons (Natural Satellites): Smaller bodies locked to a parent planet, ranging from massive spheroidal worlds with subsurface oceans to tiny, captured irregularly-shaped asteroids.
- Planetary Rings: Billions of tiny particles of ice, rock, and dust orbiting a planet inside its Roche Limit, preventing them from fusing into a proper moon.
- Asteroid Belts: Vast rings of rocky, metallic debris left over from the system’s early formation that failed to merge into a planet due to gravitational disruptions from nearby giant planets.
- Centaurs: Unstable, hybrid icy-rocky bodies that cross the orbits of the giant planets, acting as a transitional population between asteroids and comets.
- Comets & Icy Reservoirs: Small, volatile-rich bodies located at the freezing, dark outer fringes of the system (like a Kuiper Belt or a spherical Oort Cloud). When their eccentric orbits pull them close to the star, the heat vaporizes their ices, creating a visible glowing coma and tail.
- Interplanetary Medium: The sparse "empty" space filled with cosmic dust, solar wind (charged particles blasting from the star), and magnetic fields.
