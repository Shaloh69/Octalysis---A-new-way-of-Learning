<!-- block: prose -->
The board is dark.

Over the next fourteen weeks you will bring a computer up from nothing: from the
switches that hold a single bit, to the gates that add two of them, to a machine that
fetches, decodes and executes a program you wrote yourself.

This stage is orientation. Nothing here is graded.

<!-- block: callout -->
**How stages work.** Each stage opens when the ones it depends on reach 70% mastery.
A locked stage always tells you why, and how far off you are. Your instructor can open
any stage for you at any time.

<!-- block: prose -->
Three things on screen are worth naming now, because you will see them every week.

The strip along the top is the **Register Bar**. It is idle for now. Around Stage 12 it
starts showing real values from a real machine.

The gauge down the left edge is not a progress bar. It is not counting points. What it
measures is named in Stage 11, and until then it is deliberately unexplained.

The map is the course. Every connection on it is a real prerequisite, not decoration.

<!-- block: prose -->
Pick a theme and an accent colour in settings. They are yours, they follow you across
devices, and they change nothing about how you are assessed.
